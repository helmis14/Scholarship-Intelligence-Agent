International Student and Scholar Services

Funding

Western Michigan University (WMU) students are eligible for a wide range of scholarships, grants and loans that can help cover educational costs. Students are encouraged to pursue all forms of scholarships and financial aid, both from WMU and outside of WMU. Many international students inquire about financial assistance at WMU. While most U.S. federal financial aid is restricted to U.S. citizens and permanent residents, undergraduate and graduate international students may apply to other funding sources.

For a U.S. university to issue an I-20 or DS-2019, international students must provide evidence of the ability to pay for all tuition and living expenses. Western Michigan University does offer scholarships and assistantships to international undergraduate and graduate students, however. Read more to learn more about funding options, especially our Global Education Merit Scholarship.

A scholarship is an award of financial aid for a student to further their education. It can be awarded upon various criteria that might include academic merit and demonstration of financial need. Most scholarships can be combined, so we encourage you to apply for as many scholarships as possible.

The Global Education Merit Scholarship

Western Michigan University (WMU) is proud of its deep and historic commitment to international student success and well-being. We are among only 50% of public universities to offer a scholarship designed for international students.

Our Global Education Merit Scholarship, valued up to $10,000 per academic year, can be applied to your WMU tuition throughout the duration of your program.  No additional scholarship application is necessary.  A minimum 3.0 grade point average (GPA) is required.

Merit scholarships are available only for degree-seeking students on F-1 or J-1 status. They cannot be combined with governmental or non-governmental organization (NGO) scholarships or full WMU assistantships. Terms apply.

Funding Options for Undergraduate International Students

The Haenicke Institute for Global Education awards the Global Education Undergraduate Merit Scholarship to international undergraduate students admitted in the fall, spring or summer. It is awarded to international undergraduate students based on the criteria of merit and the student’s total estimated cost balance. The scholarship applies only to Western Michigan University (WMU) academic tuition. 

Scholarship Value:   

Up to $10,000 per year, disbursed in two installments for fall and spring semesters. 

Maximum Duration:   

Up to eight semesters or until graduation, whichever comes first.

Deadline to Apply:   

Complete your WMU academic admission with all application materials. No additional application is necessary. Scholarships are awarded on a rolling basis to students while funds are available.

Award Notification:  

The scholarship is awarded on a rolling basis, so students receiving the scholarship will be notified at the time of their admission to WMU or shortly afterwards.

Eligibility:   

  • Initial F-1 or J-1 international undergraduate students entering WMU for the first time after secondary school or transferring from another post-secondary institution and admitted for fall, spring or summer semester. This includes undergraduate degree Pathway programs.
  • Non-resident international status.
  • Minimum Cumulative grade point average (GPA): 3.0 out of 4.0 (WMU will convert all international grading systems to a 4-point scale)
  • Please note, we receive more eligible applicants than there are available scholarships. As such, being eligible does not guarantee you will receive the scholarship.
  • Exclusions:
    • Visa holders other than F-1/J-1 international student
    • Students fully funded for tuition through Governmental/non-governmental organization (NGO) scholarships or fully funded WMU assistantships
    • International undergraduate students applying for and enrolled in fully online degree programs (programs comprised of all online courses) 

The WMU Legacy Scholarship is intended for the children, grandchildren, stepchildren, step-grandchildren and siblings of WMU alumni. The scholarship awards $1,000 to first-time enrolled Broncos entering in fall semester.

  • Academic colleges and departments offer scholarships. Deadlines and eligibility criteria vary by colleges and departments.
  • Undergraduate students are encouraged to explore WMU Scholarship Universe for additional funding opportunities.

Funding Options for Graduate International Students

The Haenicke Institute for Global Education awards the Global Education Graduate Merit Scholarship to international graduate students admitted in the fall, spring or summer. It is awarded to international graduate students based on the criteria of merit and the student’s total estimated cost balance. The scholarship applies only to Western Michigan University (WMU) academic tuition. 

Scholarship Value:

Up to $10,000 per year, disbursed in two installments for fall and spring semesters.  

Maximum Duration:

Up to four semesters or until graduation, whichever comes first.

Deadline to Apply:

A separate application for scholarship is not needed. However, you must have a complete application to WMU by February 15 to be eligible. This includes:

  • Transcripts and test scores (including GRE [graduate record examinations]/GMAT [graduate management admission test]) as required by the program/department;
  • Recommendations;
  • Writing sample;
  • Statement of purpose;
  • Copy of resume/CV (curriculum vitae);
  • Proof of English proficiency;
  • A copy of your passport or government identification. 

Award Notification:

April

Eligibility:

  • Initial F-1 or J-1 international graduate student applicants admitted for fall, spring or summer semesters.
  • Minimum grade point average (GPA): 3.0/4.0 (WMU will convert all international grading systems to a 4-point scale).
  • Please note, we receive more eligible applicants than there are available scholarships. As such, being eligible does not guarantee you will receive the scholarship.
  • Exclusions:
    • Visa holders other than F-1/J-1 international students.
    • Students fully funded for tuition through Governmental/non-governmental organization (NGO) scholarships or fully funded WMU assistantships.
    • International graduate students applying for and enrolled in fully online degree programs (programs comprised of all online courses). 

Western Michigan University (WMU) provides opportunities for fellowships, associateships and assistantships for students planning to pursue graduate study. Collectively, these awards are known as graduate appointments. Graduate appointees are first and foremost students. As such, their most important task is to complete their degree requirements in a timely fashion; this is the primary expectation of the University. International graduate students should contact their academic department directly to inquire about the availability of graduate appointments. Information is also provided by the Graduate College and the University’s Career and Student Employment Services website. 

Graduate appointments are funded through state appropriations and federal, state, local and private grants for three principal reasons. First, employment of graduate students in teaching, research and non-teaching positions during their graduate education encourages and supports their participation in these major functions of university life and thereby strengthens the quality of the students’ educational experience. Second, appointments provide direct financial support to outstanding students who are essential to the development of quality graduate programs. Third, graduate students provide valuable and necessary services to the university in their roles as appointees. 

Applications for graduate appointments are due by February 15, and appointments are usually made by April 1 for students planning to enroll in the fall semester. Appointments are often renewable, except for Graduate College Fellowships and Dissertation Fellowships.

International students with a graduate assistantship should contact their department to inquire about special funding to cover the International Student Fee. Academic colleges, not the Haenicke Institute for Global Education, disburses applicable funds to international students who hold a graduate assistantship.

International Student Emergency Fund

The Haenicke Institute for Global Education (HIGE) provides funding to support international students in their educational attainment. The International Student Emergency Fund (ISEF) supports students who find themselves in unexpected and temporary financial hardship in order to help them continue to make progress on their degrees.

International Student Emergency Fund Request Form

A student must: 
  • Be currently enrolled in a degree program (undergraduate or graduate) or intensive English program (CELCIS) on a J-1 or F-1 visa.
  • Be in good academic standing, making adequate progress towards a degree. 
    • The ISEF committee may contact the academic department or college. 
  • Priority is given to students who are close to graduation (in their final 16-week semester), requiring only a small number of credits, and to students who can demonstrate circumstances beyond their or their family’s control.

  • Awards are typically between $500 and $2,500; in exceptional circumstances the award may be larger.
  • Costs related to college attendance expenses are a one-time award. Otherwise, students may apply once per academic year (July 1 – June 30). 
  • The award is not a scholarship.
  • Requests are considered on a rolling basis.
  • Funding is limited and not guaranteed. 

  • ISEF is not intended to replace support for expected costs of attendance or be used on an ongoing basis for routine expenses. 
  • ISEF funding is available for Emergencies and for Degree Completion Funds. Emergency funding is for urgent, unexpected financial need. For Degree Completion Funds, students must be near graduation. 
  • Submitting an application is the first step in the ISEF process and does not guarantee funding. All applications will be reviewed by a committee. Supporting documents may be required.  
  • Approval is based on available funds, students' needs, and available options. 

  • Funds are not intended to replace support for expected costs of attendance or be used for routine expenses.

Guidance for International Students: U.S. Tax Withholding on Excess Financial Aid

The drop-down menu below contains information about how your financial-aids could be impacted by U.S. taxation. For more on employment and taxation, visit our Employment and Immigration page.

As part of your financial planning while attending Western Michigan University (WMU), it’s important to understand how U.S. tax laws may affect your scholarships, grants, and assistantships, especially when the U.S. aid exceeds your qualified educational expenses. According to U.S. tax regulations:

Scholarships, grants, or other U.S. aid are not taxable if they are used for qualified education expenses, such as:

  • Tuition
  • Required fees
  • Books, supplies, and equipment required for your courses. These items must be required of all students in your course of instruction.

However, aid is taxable when used for non-qualified expenses, including but not limited to the following:

  • Housing or dorm rent
  • Meal plans or groceries
  • Travel or transportation
  • Optional equipment or personal expenses

If your total aid exceeds your qualified expenses, the excess amount may be subject to U.S. income tax and withholding. Foreign nationals in the U.S. under a F, J, M or Q visa status are taxed at 14%. All other visa statuses are taxed at 30%. 

This includes payments or discounts received by exchange and sponsored students, such as remissions, exemptions, or third-party payments. These are treated the same as scholarships and grants for tax purposes.  If the total aid exceeds your qualified expenses, the excess will be subject to U.S. income tax and withholding. 

If you are subject to this withholding, transactions reflected as “U.S. Tax on Excess U.S. Aid Funds” may appear on your WMU student account.

Western Michigan University will issue Internal Revenue Service (IRS) Form 1042-S to report taxable scholarship income for nonresident aliens. This form is used to report: 

  • The amount of scholarship, grants, or other U.S. aid subject to tax
  • Any tax withheld and remitted to the IRS on your behalf by the university 

Form 1042-S will be mailed no later than February 28th of each year. You will need this form when filing your U.S. tax return (Form 1040NR or 1040NR-EZ).

  • Keep records of your tuition charges, aid received, and documentation related to required books, supplies, and equipment.
  • File a U.S. tax return each year if you received taxable aid, even if tax was already withheld. 

You will need a Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) to file your federal tax return.

A Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) is a unique number used by the Internal Revenue Service (IRS) to track tax obligations. For most international students, this will be either: 

  • A Social Security Number (SSN) – typically issued if you are authorized to work in the U.S.
  • An Individual Taxpayer Identification Number (ITIN) – issued if you are not eligible for an SSN but still need to file a tax return. 

You must apply for a TIN before filing your tax return. The application process for an ITIN is separate and may require submitting proof of identity and foreign status. Processing times can vary, so begin this process early to avoid delays.

  1. Determine which Taxpayer Identification Number you need (Social Security Number [SSN] or Individual Taxpayer Identification Number [ITIN]).
  2. Gather required documents (passport, visa, and other supporting materials).
  3. Submit your application through the appropriate channels:
    1. SSN: Apply through the Social Security Administration.
    2. ITIN: Apply using Internal Revenue Service (IRS) Form W-7, often submitted with your tax return.

If you have had taxes withheld, or received any U.S. taxable income, you will need to file Form 1040NR by April 15th. Those who file a tax return are likely able to obtain a refund of the taxes withheld assuming they do not have a significant amount of U.S. taxable income from other U.S. sources.

The U.S. has tax treaties with many countries that may exempt some or all of your scholarship income from taxation. Treaty benefits are claimed when filing your personal tax return Internal Revenue Service (IRS) Form 1040NR. To claim a treaty benefit:

  • Review your country’s treaty details in IRS Publication 901
  • Indicate the treaty benefit when filing your tax return
  • Keep all supporting documents (passport, I-20/DS-2019, scholarship letter, etc.)

Even if exempt, you may still receive Form 1042-S and be required to file a return to report the exemption. Refer to IRS Publication 901 for details on tax treaties. 

Use Worksheet 1-1 in Internal Revenue Service (IRS) Publication 970 to determine the taxable portion of your scholarship. 

In short:

  • Add up your qualified expenses (tuition, required fees, books)
  • Subtract those from your total scholarships or grants
  • The difference = taxable income
  • Withholding federal income tax on scholarships, fellowships and grants paid to aliens
  • About Publication 519, U.S. Tax Guide for Aliens
