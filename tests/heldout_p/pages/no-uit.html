Singular courses at master's level.

How to apply: 
You apply through UiT's application website. Use the application code 9371- Singular courses at master level.
You must submit the course code (i.e. PSY-3001) in the comments field of your application. 

Access to a singular course is only given a semester at a time. You have to apply again for each semester you want to take a singular course at master's level.


Admission requirements:
Singular courses at master's level have the same admission requirements as the masterprogramme they belong to. You have to document that you meet the bachelor requirement, the special admission requirements, and the minimum grade requirement. 


Please note: The University does not offer letters of admission for visa seeking applicants to singular courses. Singular courses will not be sufficient to apply for a student visa. This letter will only be sent to our programme students. 

Not all singular courses are available for non-programme students. Information regarding availability can be found on the individual course pages here.


Application deadline:
Autumn semester: 20th of July to August 9th
Spring semester: November 1st to January 6th

Singular courses at PhD level.

How to apply:
You apply through UiT's application website. Use the application code 9301 - Singular courses at PhD level.
You must submit the course code (i.e. HEL-8040) in the comments field of your application. 


For access restricted courses:
Some courses are access restricted, these courses must be applied for again in Studentweb, after you have been admitted. The student right you receive does not automatically grant you access to restricted courses.


Courses with special admission requirements:
Contact the faculty the course belongs to in order to be assessed.


Who has to apply for admission?

  • PhD students from other institutions than UiT. You must document your PhD-status with a written confirmation from your institution.
    • Applicants with citizenship other than Norwegian, must also upload a copy of their passport.
  • Applicants that have a master's degree but aren't PhD students.
    • You must upload diploma supplement (if one was issued), transcript of records, and documentation of English requirements. 
    • Applicants with citizenship other than Norwegian, must also upload a copy of their passport.
  • PhD students at UiT that wish to take a PhD level course which is not going to be a part of their PhD degree.

Please note: The University does not offer letters of admission for visa seeking applicants to singular courses. Singular courses will not be sufficient to apply for a student visa.

Application deadline:
August 9th for the autumn semester (applications open 20th July)
January 6th for the spring semester

Average grade requirements


UiT uses the ECTS scale when calculating your average grade. 

Information regarding the ECTS credit system can be found here.
Information regarding the ECTS grading scale can be found here.

Degrees using the ECTS grading scale will be calulated with a weighted average, where a grade counts for more towards the average if the course has more credits.

For degrees using a grading scale that is not identical to that used at UiT, the applicant must be ranked on the basis of a discretionary assessment. We have internal guidelines for how to calculate such averages. Internal guidelines and the average grade will not be available for applicants. This assessment is only based on academic results, and other documentation will not be taken into consideration.

The minimum grade requirement for acceptance to most of UiT’s master programmes is an average grade of at least C (2,5). Note that some programmes do not have a grade requirement, and some require a hightened C (3,0). To see the average grade requirement for a programme see “Admission requirements” on the programme's page.

Note that a CGPA of 2,5 or 3,0 is not equivalent to a 2,5 or 3,0 in the ECTS scale.

Who must document financial ability?
International applicants (citizens of countries outside the EU/EEA) who do not already have a residence permit in Norway, must document proof of financial ability. 

Exemptions:

  • If you are a applicant residing in Norway on a work visa, under protection (asylum), with permanent residency, or under family immigration you have to upload a copy of you residence permit as proof of financial ability.
  • If you are a Norwegian citizen you are exempted from documenting proof of financial ability. Please upload a copy of your Norwegian passport as proof of citizenship.

How much is required?
International students from outside EU/EEA must have access to enough funds to pay for their own living expenses for at least one academic year. If admitted, these funds must be transferred to a student deposit account in Norway to apply for student visa.

You must document NOK 170 368* (for admission autmn 2027) or its equivalent in foreign currency as proof of financial ability for at least one academic year (2 academic semesters). This amount is set by The Norwegian Directorate of Immigration (UDI). 

*The required amount is subject to change by April each year.

Required documentation:
If financial ability is not documented, the application for admission will be rejected, regardless of academic qualifications. 

Documentation of financial ability should not be older than three months at the time of the application, and must be uploaded to the application web within the application deadline. Financial ability can be documented with one of the following documents:

  • Self-funded applicants: Please upload your recent official bank statement/transcript in your name showing possession of the required amount of money.
  • Applicants with scholarships or student loan: Please upload the official letter confirming that you are eligible for a scholarship/student loan sufficient of the required amount from the funding or educational authorities in your home country.
  • Sponsored applicants: If you are sponsored by another person, you must document the following:
    • Sponsor´s recent official bank statement/transcript.
    • Sponsor's guarantee letter where the sponsor confirms that he/she is willing to transfer the required amount to the student deposit. The letter must include sponsor's full name, the applicant's name, sponsor's signature, sponsor's postal address/email address.

Who can sponsor you?
A sponsor is a relative, employer, or friend (self-funded applicants) or an organisation (applicants with loan/scholarships from home country) who are willing to finance your living costs during your studies.

Please note that UiT cannot help you find a sponsor or scholarships.

After arriving to Norway, you must register at the local police station within seven days.



When do you need to register with the police?
Non-EU/EEA: The Norwegian Directorate of Norwegian Immigration (UDI) requires that that you apply for a Study Permit and register with the Police within seven days of arriving to Norway. Log in to UDI's Application Portal and make a "follow-up"- appointment with the police.

EU/EEA: The Norwegian Directorate of Norwegian Immigration (UDI) requires that that you register with the Police if you are staying more than three months in Norway. Book an appointment with the Police.

What to bring when you meet with the police

  • Your offer of admission from UiT (found in the application portal)
  • Your visa letter from UDI (non-EU/EEA students)
  • Your housing contract (tenancy agreement)
  • Your address in Norway with the postal code (see your tenancy agreement)
  • The housing code (see your tenancy agreement)
  • Your passport
  • Health Insurance: Insurance & health matters - international students | UiT


Norwegian identity number:
The Norwegian national identity number is used in many contexts in Norway, including in communication with public authorities, banks and telephone service providers, and for a variety of subscription services. You must apply for a Norwegian Identity number shortly after arriving in Norway. National Identity numbers are issued by the Norwegian Tax Administration to all persons residing in Norway.

Foreigners intending to stay in Norway for less than six months are issued a temporary national identity number (D number). Your national identity number or D number will appear on your tax deduction card.

Deposit account for international students


Proof of financial ability is a formal admission requirements at UiT, for non-EU/EEA applicants. The Norwegian Directorate of Immigration (UDI) requires that you must have access to enough funds to pay for own living expenses for at least one academic year, to be granted a study permit.

The Arctic Student Welfare Organization of Norway (Studentsamskipnaden) have a deposit account in Danske Bank for foreign students, where they can deposit the required amount of money. You will have access to your money after arriving in Norway through a prepaid card (cash card) until you receive a national ID number to open your own bank account. The deposit will only be transferred to your bank account or to your cash card (se below for detailed information).

Self-funded students must transfer their funds to the deposit account to be able to apply for a student visa (Study Permit). The deadline for transfer is June 1st.

If you live in Norway and have your own Norwegian bank account, you do not need to transfer any fund to the deposit account.

ECTS Credit system


UiT The Arctic University of Norway uses a system of credits (studiepoeng) for measuring study activities, considered equivalent to the European Credit Transfer and Accumulation System (ECTS). A full year of studying is normally equivalent to 60 ECTS, around 1500-1800 hours of study. One semester is normally equivalent to 30 ECTS. The academic year normally lasts for 10 months and runs from the beginning of August to the end of June.

The workload of each course is based on an estimate of the total time required for completing studies and exams (lectures, seminars, readings, individual preparation etc.). There is no exact national or institutional standard for measuring of workload. Workload estimates are based on custom and tradition within each academic field.

Degree structure


Short programmes at UiT consist of 60 ECTS. 

Bachelor degrees at UiT are awarded after the equivalent of three years of full-time study (180 ECTS).

Master degrees at UiT are obtained after two years of study (120 ECTS) or (90 ECTS). A master’s degree programme includes a thesis of between 30 and 60 ECTS.

The Doctoral degree Philosophiae Doctor is normally obtained after three or four years of study. You will find more information on PhD programmes here.

Assessment methods


A number of assessment methods are in use at UiT. The most common of which are written examinations, oral examinations, portfolio assessments, home examinations/assignments and joint project papers written in collaboration with fellow students.

Please visit our page regarding examinations at UiT for more information.

Grading system


Grades for bachelor and master examinations at UiT are awarded according to a graded scale from A (highest) to F (lowest), with E as the minimum pass grade. Grades are awarded according to the following criteria:

Grade Description Evaluation criteria
A Excellent An excellent performance, clearly outstanding. The candidate demonstrates excellent judgement and a high degree of independent thinking.
B Very good A very good performance. The candidate demonstrates sound judgement and a very good degree of independent thinking.
C Good A good performance in most areas. The candidate demonstrates a reasonable degree of judgement and independent thinking in the most important areas.
D Satisfactory A satisfactory performance, but with significant shortcomings. The candidate demonstrates a limited degree of judgement and independent thinking.
E Sufficient A performance that meets the minimum criteria, but no more. The candidate demonstrates a very limited degree of judgement and independent thinking.
F Fail A performance that does not meet the minimum academic criteria. The candidate demonstrates an absence of both judgement and independent thinking.

 

For some examinations a pass/fail grade is given.

If your application is rejected, or if you are put on a waiting list due to limited number of places available, you have the right to ask for grounds of rejection and appeal the decision.

IMPORTANT: It is your responsibility as an applicant to provide all required documents within the deadline. Unless you have received an email from us stating a specific deadline, the deadline to submit any kind of documents is the same as the application deadline for the program. Please make sure to check your registered email for any important messages from us after the application deadline. This includes the spam filter in your email account. If the reason for rejecting your application is that you have not provided all required documents within the given deadline, your appeal will be disregarded.

According to the Norwegian Public Administration Act, § 24, you have the right to ask for grounds for the decision. The time limit is three weeks from the day you receive the email with the decision.
If you ask for grounds of rejection or appeal, we will review your application again, to see if there might have been an error when processing your application. If an error has been made, you will receive a new letter with results of your application. If the results of your application are correct, the first rejection is valid.
According to the Norwegian Public Administration Act, § 28, you have the right to appeal the decision. The time limit is three weeks from the day you receive the email with the decision. In case of appeal please specify your reasons for appealing the decision.

NOTE: We will always treat your first response as a question of grounds for the decision. If you receive grounds for the decision, there will be a new deadline to appeal the decision, three weeks from the date you receive the grounds. This is according to the Norwegian Public Administration Act § 29.

An appeal should sent to the admissions office by email. The appeal must be signed by you or your proxy. If your proxy is not an attorney, you must attach a written power of attorney. In your appeal you should give grounds for your appeal in accordance with the Norwegian Public Administration Act § 32.

The legislation on export control is intended to help prevent the proliferation of weapons of mass destruction and their means of delivery. At UiT, this affects the assessment of applications for admission to certain master's degree programmes, as well as certain singular courses on master's and PhD level.

Export controls are applied to countries where there is a justified suspicion or specific information about activities relating to the development and use of weapons of mass destruction. The legislation covers the transfer of knowledge. The purpose is to prevent educational institutions from inadvertently contributing knowledge to the development or use of weapons of mass destruction or delivery systems for such weapons.

UiT may deny admission in disciplines that are particularly relevant to the development and use of weapons of mass destruction, in accordance with Norway's Export Control Act and Export Control Regulations.

Yes, it is not a problem if you have a gap between your previous degree and your new studies.

Am I eligible for admission if I graduate after the application deadline?


If you are an non-EU/EEA applicant: No, you need to have completed your education within the application deadline, if the education you are completing is relevant to your application. We do not accept provisional certificates. Conditional admission is not possible for applicants that must apply for student visa, read more here.

If you are an applicant from EU/EEA: Yes, but only for admission to masters degree programmes. If you are completing your current degree after the application deadline and do not need a visa to come to Norway, you may be granted conditional admission to master's degree programmes, read more here.

What documents do I need to upload after I apply?


An overview of the documents you need to submit is provided on our page for international admissions.

All applicants must upload the following documents:

  • Copy of your passport, or EU national ID card
  • Enclosure form
  • Documents showing change of name (if this applies to you)
  • Proof of sufficient English language proficiency
  • Relevant educational diploma and transcript
  • Diploma supplement (if you have received one)
  • All documents must be uploaded in their original language, as well as an official translation into English or a Scandinavian language. Self-translated documents, or AI translated documents, are not accepted
  • Documents for programme specific admission requirements
  • Proof of financial ability (only for non-EU/EEA applicants)
  • See countries with additional requirements here

Will you confirm that you received my documentation?


Yes, when you submit a new document, you will receive an automatically generated receipt.

It is also possible to check that your documentation has been uploaded successfully by logging in to the online application portal. 

Can I upload documents after the deadline?


​​In principle, the deadlines for submission of documentation are absolute, and your application for admission will be rejected if we do not receive the correct and complete documentation within the given deadline. This also includes the results of the English language test. If you know that you will receive important documentation after the given deadline, you must contact us, and we will try to find a solution.

Can I delete documents after I have submitted them?


You can delete documents right after uploading them, but it is not possible to delete documents when we have started processing your application. 

Can I upload attachments to my application electronically?


Yes, you should upload your documentation to the online application portal, where you submitted your application for admission. Documentation includes diplomas, transcripts, English language test results etc.

Do you require a minimum grade point average (GPA) for admission?


Yes, the general admission requirements (average grade requirements) stipulate the minimum requirements for admission.

If we receive a large number of applicants that exceed the minimum GPA you will not be guaranteed entry even though you meet the minimum requirements.

How are applicants ranked?


If there are more qualified applicants then there are available seats in a programme, we have to rank all qualified applicants. Applicants are only ranked on the basis of their GPA, and not by statement of purpose, letter of motivation, personal interest, letter of recommendation, or work experience.

How do I document special admission requirements?


Some programmes have special admission requirements that must be met in order to qualify. The following are further specifications of some common misunderstandings regarding these requirements. 

Minimum 80 ECTS in a relevant field:

  • Must be documented with a transcript of record that includes full course name, course code (if available), credit amount for each course, grade for each course
  • Course description for all the relevant courses

If we are unsure whether the applicant's bachelor's degree can cover the special admission requirement for the master program, we will receive an assessment from the academic staff at the relevant Department.


Drivers Licence:

  • Both sides of the physical licence are required. We do not accept digital licences


Relevant practical experience, document must include:

  • Start and end dates of the employment/activity
  • Employment percentage or the number of hours worked
  • Description of employment/activity, this must be a seperate letter if not included in the employment letter
  • A signature. We accept digital signature embeded in the document


Motivation letter / Statement of Purpose:

  • Only required for the master's in Peace and Conflict Transformation
  • There are no guidelines for what to include in the letter

When do I receive the results of the admission?


Non-EU/EEA applicants that applied by the deadline of November 15th, will receive the results of their applications within the end of February.

EU/EEA applicants that applied by the deadline of March 1st, will receive the results of their applications within the middle of May.

Both non-EU/EEA and EU/EEA applicants that applied for master in Fine Art and master in Landscape Architecture will also receive their results within the middle of May.

I have received an offer of admission from UiT. What do I do now?


Please accept your offer in the application portal and start planning by visiting our website "After admission​". 

If you are waiting for an offer from a different university we recommend that you still accept the offer at UiT within the given deadline. You can withdraw at any time by contacting us. 

I was offered a place on a waiting list. What does this mean?


If you are on a waiting list, it means that you are qualified, but there are more qualified applicants than there are available seats. If you want to stay on the waiting list you have to accept the offer in the application portal within the given deadline. 

If you accept the waiting list offer you may be offered admission at a later date if a seat opens up.

You will not receive an e-mail if your waiting list number changes, you will only receive an e-mail if you are offered admission. If you are offered admission you have to accept the new offer within the given deadline.

If you are put on a waiting list you have the right to ask for grounds of rejection.

How do I ask for grounds of rejection?


If your application is rejected, or if you are put on a waiting list due to limited number of available places, you have the right to ask for grounds of rejection and appeal the decision. More information on "How to appeal".

Can I bring my spouse and/or children to Norway?


Your spouse or cohabitant and children can usually apply to come and live with you in Norway.
