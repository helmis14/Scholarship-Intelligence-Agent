TitleTop-up Scholarship: Optimising forest monitoring programs in Southwest WA forests
CourseDoctor of Philosophy
Project opportunityOptimising forest monitoring programs in Southwest WA forests
Description

This project will focus on the amazing and diverse forests of Southwest WA. These forests are monitored regularly to understand how they are changing over time and being impacted by changing climates and human activities. However, designing effective forest monitoring programs is challenging. Monitoring programs must balance sampling effort at individual sites with broad spatial coverage across a range of landscape positions and vegetation and soil types, adequate replication, and an appropriate frequency of repeat surveys. For instance, intensive site level surveys may provide little information beyond the specific location, while limiting the number of locations and time points that can be monitored. Conversely, a monitoring program that prioritises frequent sampling and broad spatial coverage at the expense of sufficient resolution may fail to detect important ecological changes, while still imposing substantial logistical demands. The optimal balance among these trade-offs will vary according to the ecological processes and threats being monitored, each of which may require different sampling designs, spatial scales, and survey frequencies for effective detection and assessment. Differences in forest type, soils, vegetation structure and disturbance history may also affect the performance of different survey methods.  The objective of this project is to evaluate how vegetation monitoring design affects the capacity to characterise vegetation condition and detect priority ecological changes across Southwest WA forests.

We are looking for an enthusiastic PhD candidate to tackle this project. You should have some skills and background in statistics, data analysis and experiential or survey design, and a keen interest in applying those skills to conservation, ecology and the natural environment. The project will be supervised by researchers from The University of Western Australia and from the Department of Biodiversity, Conservation and Attractions of Western Australia.

Keywordsforests, ecology, statistics, survey design
