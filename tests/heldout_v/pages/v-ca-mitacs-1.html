ABOUT THE AWARDS

Now in their 16th year, the Mitacs Innovation Awards celebrate the people and partnerships turning research, talent, and ambition into real-world results.

From breakthrough research and inclusive innovation to start-up growth, commercialization, mentorship, and industry collaboration, the awards recognize the achievements that help build a stronger and more resilient Canadian economy.

Each year, the Mitacs Innovation Awards bring together leaders from research, business, post-secondary institutions, government, and the broader innovation ecosystem to celebrate the impact of Mitacs-supported work across Canada.

WHY HONOUR INNOVATION?

Canada is competing for what comes next. The economies that succeed will be built on ambition, speed, and talent that can transform research into results.

The Mitacs Innovation Awards celebrate that work in action.

They are an opportunity to:

Recognize outstanding achievements and share compelling stories of Canadian ingenuity
✓
Celebrate the students, postdoctoral fellows, professors, entrepreneurs, and partners helping turn ideas into impact

Bring together leaders from industry, post-secondary institutions, government, and the broader innovation ecosystem
✓
Show how Mitacs-supported projects are helping Canada innovate faster and grow stronger

2026 Award categories

Outstanding Innovator

Recognizes four individual Mitacs program participants (students or postdoctoral fellows) whose research shows exceptional potential for broader societal or economic impact.

Inclusive Innovator

Recognizes a Mitacs program participant (students or postdoctoral fellows) who is advancing equity and inclusion within Canada’s innovation ecosystem.

Canadian Enterprise Innovator

Recognizes a Mitacs industry partner whose leadership, collaborative approach, and support for research and development demonstrate a strong commitment to advancing Canadian innovation.

Outstanding Research Leadership

Presented to an academic supervisor who has demonstrated excellence in mentorship, research leadership, and industry collaboration.

Canadian Start-Up Innovator

Celebrates a Mitacs program participant (students or postdoctoral fellows) who has successfully launched a start-up, turning innovative research into business growth and market impact.

Meet last year’s winners

Eleven talented individuals and organizations were awarded for their impactful work across five categories.

With four winners in this category, the Mitacs Outstanding Innovator Award recognizes extraordinary talent from across Canada among students and postdoctoral fellows who have participated in a Mitacs program.

This category emphasizes the societal and economic impact of research, as well as its potential to drive innovation, strengthen communities, and address real-world challenges.

Dr. Nicoletta Faraone
Associate Professor, Chemistry Department, and
Head of Canadian Tick Research and Innovation Centre,
Acadia University

Dr. Faraone led development of an all-natural and long-lasting fabric spray tick repellent. The first-of-its-kind product offers an alternative to synthetic insect repellents like DEET.

Dr. Nello David Sansone
Postdoctoral Researcher, Multifunctional
Composites Manufacturing Laboratory,
University of Toronto

Dr. Sansone designed lightweight alternatives to the industry-standard glass-filled polypropylene (plastic) materials used by car manufacturers. With these new materials, manufacturers can design cars that use less energy to drive longer distances.

Discover the Winning Work

Dr. Tae-Ho Kim
Postdoctoral Fellow, Biomechatronic Systems Laboratory, Simon Fraser University

Dr. Kim designed a first-of-its-kind, self-powered continuous blood pressure (BP) monitor that provides measurements every few seconds. The device saves physicians the time it takes to collect multiple readings from traditional arm cuff BP monitors.

Discover the Winning Work

Dr. Sara Imani
Postdoctoral Researcher, McMaster University

Dr. Imani developed a coating that can reduce infection risk on surfaces in high-traffic areas such as hospitals, schools, and airports. The coating has demonstrated more than 99 percent efficacy at killing pathogens.

Discover the Winning Work

Nitesh Sanghai
PhD Candidate, College of Pharmacy, University of Manitoba

Sanghai’s promising new ALS drug brings hope for a better quality and longer life to the 3,000–4,000 Canadians living with the disease. The drug has shown potential to delay disease onset, prevent ALS-induced weight loss, and extend life.

Discover the Winning Work

The Inclusive Innovator Award celebrates students and postdoctoral fellows who have participated in a Mitacs program and who are from equity-deserving groups, helping to develop and foster a more inclusive innovation ecosystem.

It recognizes research achievements that promote social inclusion, incorporate diverse perspectives, and prioritize accessibility in the design and execution of innovative projects, products, and services.

Dr. Anne Laurie
Postdoctoral Researcher, Department of Education (Child Studies), Concordia University

Dr. Laurie’s work on an equitable Developmental Language Disorder (DLD) assessment tool has provided a culturally sensitive way to distinguish language differences from DLD, promoting early intervention for children who need it while avoiding mislabeling.

Discover the Winning Work

 

The Canadian Start-Up Innovator Award celebrates an outstanding Canadian innovator who has participated in the Mitacs Accelerate Entrepreneur program and has used that support to help launch or grow their entrepreneurial journey.

This award highlights the successful commercialization of research or the creation of a new business enterprise by a Mitacs program participant. It recognizes the potential created when research, ambition, and entrepreneurship align.

Dr. Narjes Allahrabbi
Postdoctoral Researcher, Electrical, Computer and Biomedical Engineering Department, Toronto Metropolitan University

Dr. Allahrabbi’s work on a novel sperm enhancement device offers a more affordable and accessible alternative to in vitro fertilization (IVF). Her Toronto-based start-up, Fertilead, is currently developing clinical and at-home versions of the technology.

Fattah Haeri
Biomedical Engineer, Femera Med Tech

Haeri’s start-up company, Femera, is commercializing a vaginal probe that restores tissue elasticity and strengthens pelvic floor muscles, offering women experiencing urinary incontinence a more affordable, accessible, and minimally invasive alternative to surgery.

The Canadian Enterprise Innovator Award celebrates a Mitacs industry partner whose leadership, adoption of emerging technology, and support for research and development demonstrate an ongoing commitment to innovation, talent, productivity, and economic growth.

This award recognizes partner organizations helping Canada compete more ambitiously and turn ideas into measurable results.

Hydro-Québec
Electricity Utility, Quebec

Through its innovation hub — and with the help of hundreds of Mitacs interns — Hydro-Québec is developing next-generation technologies to improve grid reliability, integrate renewable energy sources, and accelerate the transition from fossil fuels.

The Outstanding Research Leadership Award is presented to an academic supervisor with an exemplary record of developing collaborations with business partners.

Associated with a university, college, CEGEP, or polytechnic, the winner of this category provides valuable research and training experiences to Mitacs interns and supports impactful projects that connect talent, research, and industry.

Dr. Lucas Hof
Mechanical Engineering Professor and Director of Laboratory for Smart and Circular Manufacturing, École de technologie supérieure (ÉTS)

With support from more than 40 Mitacs interns over six years, Dr. Hof has developed innovations ranging from recyclable 3D-printed shoes and intelligent industrial safety systems to flexible solar panels and manufacturing processes that repurpose textile waste.

Faramarz Samavati
Computer Science Professor,
University of Calgary

Dr. Samavati’s made-in-Canada 3D digital earth system provides an alternative to traditional geographic information systems like Google Maps that represent the Earth as flat.

Help us recognize innovation at your institution

Post-secondary leaders, Vice-Presidents of Research, Offices of Research teams, and institutional partners play an important role in helping us identify outstanding Mitacs-supported projects across Canada.

If you know a student, postdoctoral fellow, professor, start-up founder, entrepreneur, partner organization, or research team whose work deserves national recognition, we encourage you to submit a nomination or share this opportunity through your institutional channels.

Self and peer nominations are accepted in all categories.

 

The nomination period runs from August 4 to September 8, 2026.

CELEBRATE WITH US

The 2026 Mitacs Innovation Awards will take place on November 23, 2026, at the National Arts Centre in Ottawa.

We look forward to celebrating the individuals and teams behind the projects delivering a return on innovation for Canada. Further event details will be shared closer to the date.

Questions

Important nomination notes

  • Some categories require letters of recommendation. Please review the nomination requirements for your selected category before submitting.
  • Nominations for the Outstanding Innovator Award and Inclusive Innovator Award require a letter of recommendation from either the nominee’s academic supervisor or partner organization contact associated with the Mitacs project.
  • Nominations for the Outstanding Research Leadership Award require letters of recommendation from a representative sample of the nominee’s peers, past/current collaborators, and/or students/ postdoctoral fellows previously supervised by the nominee. A competitive nomination package typically includes 3 or more letters.
  • Additional requirements may apply by category.
