Doctoral Grants & Scientific Missions Programme

Since its establishment, the National Council for Scientific Research of Lebanon (CNRS-L) has been committed to supporting and promoting the Doctoral Grants Programme, with the aim of assisting Lebanese students who wish to pursue their higher studies, in line with the Council’s vision and orientations toward building academic and research capacities across all fields, particularly those addressing pressing societal challenges.


For many years, the Council relied on foreign scholarship programmes, before the establishment of doctoral schools within Lebanese universities, through bilateral agreements with European universities and institutions. When the opportunity arose, the Council concluded, in addition to these external agreements, several partnerships with local universities to fund a significant number of PhD theses each year, to be conducted entirely in Lebanon or jointly with foreign institutions.


The Doctoral Grants Programme was relaunched in 1999 with sixteen scholarships, most of them at French universities. By 2024, the number of newly awarded and renewed grants under various cooperation agreements reached twenty scholarships, while the total number of grants awarded between 1999 and 2024 exceeded 700.


At the beginning of 2020, due to the crises that struck Lebanon and the collapse of the national currency, some agreements were suspended or adjusted to adapt to the new circumstances, ensuring the availability of adequate funding to cover the expenses of doctoral students.

The Council has concluded joint doctoral funding agreements with major Lebanese universities: the Lebanese University (2009), the American University of Beirut (2012), Saint Joseph University (2013), and the Holy Spirit University of Kaslik (2013). At the request of the American University of Beirut, the Council approved in 2015 an increase in the number of jointly funded doctoral grants to 14 per year. In the same year, the Council signed a cooperation agreement with the Beirut Arab University, which entered into effect during the 2015–2016 academic year. In 2016, the Council approved a joint-funding agreement with the Lebanese American University for several doctoral grants starting in the fall of 2016 and decided to increase the number of joint grants with Saint Joseph University from 8 to 12 per year, beginning in the 2016–2017 academic year. It is worth noting that the Council used to allocate several doctoral grants annually to its five national centers: the National Centre for Geophysical Research, the National Centre for Marine Sciences, the National Center for Natural Hazards and Early Warning, the National Centre for Remote Sensing, and the Lebanese Atomic Energy Commission. Both CNRS-L staff members (whether permanent or contractual) and external applicants could benefit from this programme, provided they met all the eligibility criteria established by the Council.

At the beginning of 2020, due to the financial and economic circumstances in Lebanon, the implementation of all these agreements was suspended until sufficient resources, either from the Council or from the partner universities, became available to enable their reactivation. At the beginning of 2024, the program was reinstated, and all Terms of Reference (TOR) agreements were re-signed with the partner universities.

Since 2020, the Council has maintained joint programmes with foreign institutions that ensure adequate financial support for doctoral students, particularly during their research stays abroad. Accordingly, the CNRS-L continues to fund two doctoral grants with the University of Montpellier (Université de Montpellier – UM) and four grants (instead of two) with the University of Littoral Côte d’Opale (Université du Littoral Côte d’Opale – ULCO). In the same year, and in collaboration with the Agence universitaire de la Francophonie (AUF), the Council launched a new integrated programme for funding both research projects and doctoral grants within these financed projects. Under this agreement, five projects and corresponding doctoral grants are funded annually, and AUF increased its contribution to €8,000 per project, including the student’s grant. Most Lebanese universities benefit from these bilateral programmes, whereby doctoral research is jointly conducted between local laboratories (funded by the CNRS-L for six months per year) and laboratories of the French or Francophone partner institutions (funded by the partner university or AUF for six months per year). As for the CNRS-L research centres, the previous model based on full funding by the Council was replaced by a new funding agreement with the French Embassy (SAFAR Programme). This programme exclusively benefits CNRS-L research centres, under which doctoral projects are carried out jointly between the Council’s laboratories (funded by CNRS-L for six months per year) and French institutions (funded by the French Embassy for six months per year). Given the depreciation of the national currency, the Council increased its financial contribution under all these agreements, raising the funding allocated to doctoral students for their six-month research period in Lebanon from LBP 4.5 million to LBP 270 million in 2024. Thus, the active agreements for the 2024–2025 academic year are limited to four partnerships: CNRS-L/ULCO, CNRS-L/UM, CNRS-L/AUF, and CNRS-L/French Embassy. Doctoral research topics fall within the following domains: environment, air pollution, water pollution, nutrition, sustainable water management, renewable energy, natural hazards, and medical sciences, in addition to themes related to crisis management and others aligned with the scientific priorities of the CNRS-L research centres.

Applicants for the Council’s doctoral grants must meet the following requirements:

  • Nationality: The applicant must have been a Lebanese citizen for at least ten years.
  • Age limit: The applicant must not exceed thirty (30) years of age when applying for the first year of the PhD.
  • Academic qualification: The applicant must hold a Master’s degree (or its equivalent) from a university recognized by the Council.
  • The total duration of university studies leading to the Bachelor’s or Diploma degree, together with the Master’s (or equivalent), must not be less than five years.
  • The field of study must be relevant to the proposed PhD research topic.
  • The applicant’s grades in the Master’s (or equivalent) must meet at least the following thresholds:
  • In Lebanese universities applying the American system, or in European countries other than France: 85 / 100
  • In Lebanese universities applying the French system (M1 + M2): 70 / 100
  • In French universities (M1 + M2): 70 / 100
  • In Canadian or U.S. universities (M.Sc.): GPA ≥ 3.5 / 4 or 4.25 / 5

In addition to the above qualifications, CNRS-L grants are subject to the following conditions:

  • Non-duplication of funding: A CNRS-L grant holder may not benefit from another scholarship or funding source outside the Council’s framework. Exceptionally, the Council may approve additional income upon documented justification and in accordance with its regulations.

Year of study: The grant is awarded to candidates in their first or second year of doctoral studies.

  • Compliance: If the grantee fails to meet the obligations or conditions of the Council’s Doctoral Grants Programme, the grant shall be suspended, and the grantee must reimburse all funds previously received from the Council.
  • Completeness of application: Only applications that are complete and fully documented will be considered.
  • Duration and renewal: The grant is awarded for one year, upon the Secretary-General’s recommendation and the Board of Directors’ approval. It is renewable for a maximum period of three years, subject to periodic progress reports summarizing the research activities, accompanied by a support letter from the academic supervisor.
  • Acknowledgment: The Council must be acknowledged as a funding body in all publications and scientific contributions related to the PhD research.
  • Ethics compliance: Both the supervisor in Lebanon and the grantee must adhere to the CNRS-L Code of Research Ethics.

This programme aims to strengthen scientific cooperation between Lebanon and Québec through the support of joint research and the exchange of expertise between researchers from both sides. It is considered one of the most important bilateral initiatives contributing to the consolidation of academic and research partnerships at the international level, in line with the CNRS-L’s priorities to promote scientific openness and the development of research capacities. The programme was launched following the cooperation agreement signed in July 2022 between the National Council for Scientific Research in Lebanon (CNRS-L), the Fonds de recherche du Québec (FRQ), and the Agence universitaire de la Francophonie (AUF). Since its launch, thirty scientific missions have been carried out between Lebanon and Québec, significantly contributing to the strengthening of collaboration among research teams in both countries. The programme offers short-term research missions for Lebanese researchers to Québec, lasting between two and four months, at various universities in Québec. The research domains include natural and engineering sciences, social and human sciences, and health sciences. The financial support for these missions is fully covered by the Fonds de recherche du Québec (FRQ), with no financial contribution required from the CNRS-L or AUF. The package includes a round-trip ticket to Canada and a monthly allowance of 3,000 Canadian dollars. Eligible applicants are researchers affiliated with Lebanese universities or research centers under the CNRS-L, holding a Ph.D. degree. Priority is given to projects that demonstrate high scientific quality and a clear impact on the development of research in Lebanon and Québec.
