COMMUNITY FELLOWSHIP

We're accepting applications for the CIP Community Fellowship's 2026-27 cohort through April 30, 2026.

 

Program Overview

The CIP Community Fellowship program fosters and grows a network of practitioners with the goal of building collaboration, sharing ideas, creating new initiatives, and nurturing a more interconnected community that is passionate about the CIP’s mission to resist strategic misinformation, promote an informed society, and strengthen democratic discourse. Through experiential learning and practical application, the program aims to provide practitioners in wide-ranging fields, such as journalism, education, technology, librarianship, government, community organizations and law, with opportunities to explore the intersection between research and professional experience. 

The CIP Community Fellowship is designed as an opportunity for participating Fellows to: 

  • Be part of a vibrant, multidisciplinary research community to explore new ideas.
  • Gain knowledge about the current landscape of mis/disinformation and various intervention practices and initiatives. 
  • Build connections and engage with CIP-affiliated researchers, staff, and students. 
  • Participate in center events and projects.
  • Benefit from a CIP mentor.
  • Showcase their projects through CIP events. 
  • Bring new perspectives, insights, and connections back into their organizations, networks and communities.

By participating in the program, Fellows will be better equipped to help their communities and institutions navigate our complex information environments.

Choose Your Track

PROJECT TRACK

Who are you?

A professional working in the U.S. from a field impacted by misinformation (such as journalism, education, technology, librarianship, government, community organizations and law) who feels the fellowship would uniquely inspire or energize your work and who would contribute new insights and experiences to the CIP Fellowship cohort and broader CIP community. 

What will you do?

You will lead a strategic and impactful project related to CIP’s mission. This might include the development of new programs, technologies, or other activities that help individuals, communities, or institutions better navigate their information environment. 

Past projects include:

  • A social studies teacher working with a CIP scholar to create The Feed Game to help students understand the tactics and incentives for sharing information on social media.
  • A librarian creating and implementing a program to help teens navigate wellness and fitness information online while learning from experts in exercise science.
  • An exploration of how antitrust enforcement may better achieve its goals by considering the impact of misinformation on digital markets.
  • Research on the use of deepfake technology in the media during the 2024 Indonesian elections.

Expectations

  • Attend a 2-day Misinformation Workshop (in person) the week of August 12-13, 2026 at the University of Washington’s Seattle campus (travel & accommodation paid for and/or arranged by the CIP)
  • Connect with CIP mentor once a month to share updates and get support (in-person or remote)  
  • Connect with CIP Education & Engagement manager once a quarter to share updates and get support (remote)  
  • Attend and participate in 4 monthly cohort meetings (March-June, evenings, remote)
  • Attend and participate in CIP’s weekly meetings when possible (hybrid format)
  • Present at least once at CIP’s weekly meeting (in-person or remote)
  • Complete or demonstrate substantial progress on your proposed project through documented milestones. 
  • Submit a summary report of your Fellowship experience.

Financial Support 

All selected candidates will receive a $5,000 stipend, distributed over the course of the Fellowship appointment, to support expenses related to their project work. The CIP will also cover travel expenses associated with the Fellowship experience.

EDUCATOR COHORT TRACK

Who are you?

An educator — teacher, librarian, or otherwise — working in a school, library, college, university, or other educational organization in the U.S. who is passionate about helping others navigate their information environment through formal or informal learning and would contribute new insights and experiences to the CIP Fellowship cohort and broader CIP community. 

What will you do?

You will plan and implement a project inspired by existing CIP resources, research, or programs that teaches a specific audience to better navigate their information environment.

This could look like a teacher, librarian, or other educator:

  • Adapting and implementing a CIP program or activity for a specific audience.
  • Translating CIP research into educational activities, workshops, or programs and implementing for a specific audience.
  • Experimenting with a novel way to reach your audience through CIP-inspired information literacy programming.
  • Integrating information literacy skills in a grade, subject, program, or with a specific population where they are currently lacking. 
  • Training staff members to implement programs / lessons across an institution or department.

Expectations

  • Attend a 2-day Misinformation Workshop (in person) the week of August 12-13, 2026 at the University of Washington’s Seattle campus (travel & accommodation paid for and/or arranged by the CIP)
  • Connect with CIP Education & Engagement Manager once a month to share updates and get support (remote)  
  • Attend and participate in 4 monthly cohort meetings (March-June, evenings, remote)
  • Attend and participate in CIP’s weekly meetings when possible (hybrid format)
  • Complete and implement your project.
  • Submit a summary report of your Fellowship experience.

Financial Support 

All selected candidates will receive a $2,000 stipend, distributed over the course of the Fellowship appointment, to support expenses related to their project work. The CIP will also cover travel expenses associated with the Fellowship experience.

Eligibility & Qualifications (Both Tracks)

We welcome interest and applications from professionals working in the U.S. who feel that CIP’s Community Fellowship would uniquely inspire or energize their work. We encourage applications from those working in a broad range of fields that are impacted by misinformation, and who would contribute new insights and experiences to the Fellows cohort and broader CIP community. 

Consider applying to the Community Fellowship program if you are: 

  • A professional or scholar-practitioner with at least 3 years of experience  addressing mis/disinformation; 
  • Passionate about helping individuals, communities and institutions navigate our complex information environments; 
  • Comfortable with self-guided work and can independently develop and execute projects within program deadlines;
  • Able to attend the Misinformation Workshop on the week of August 12-13, 2026 in Seattle (CIP will pay for and/or arrange your travel, accommodation, and per diem).

Fellowship selection is based on a number of factors, including: a commitment to the public interest, the potential to advance the field, the capacity to diversify and broaden the CIP’s community, and the degree to which the fellowship has the potential to accelerate the candidate’s impact and contribute to their ongoing development. We will also be striving to assemble a cohort that we believe offers the greatest potential for mutual learning. Unfortunately, we cannot accommodate international applicants for the 2026-27 cohort.

Application 

The following items are required in the application for consideration:

  • Current resume or CV
  • Project track proposal or Educator track proposal

Frequently Asked Questions

What do you look for in applications?

Here are some things we look for in applications:

Mission-aligned: 

  • The project has a direct connection to helping individuals, communities or institutions navigate our complex information environments, and your application clearly demonstrates this mission. 

Fit: 

  • The project clearly needs or would benefit from CIP’s expertise in the information environment. Projects and programs that are already well-established without a clear new need for CIP expertise are less likely to be chosen. 
  • Project fellows are assigned a CIP mentor whose expertise aligns with their project. You can identify a preferred mentor on your application, though you don’t have to, and most applications don’t. However, you should make sure your application aligns with the CIP’s work in general, as we must be able to identify a staff or faculty member who would be a good mentorship fit to approve your project.

Feasibility: 

  • The scope of the project is realistic for the fellowship term. Nine months goes by quickly! This is not a good fit for launching a startup or creating a new year-long curriculum. 
  • This is a community fellowship, and we find the projects that are most successful aim to impact a specific group or community, not a general audience like the public.
  • If your project involves working with groups or organizations outside the CIP, you should already have a relationship with those groups and confirm their willingness to participate before submitting your application. 

What differentiates the project track from the educator track?

  • The project track is open to anyone whose work aligns with CIP’s mission while the educator track is only open to educators. 
  • The project track pairs fellows with a CIP mentor (faculty or staff) who has expertise related to their project. The educator track pairs fellows with the CIP Education & Engagement manager. For both tracks, connections with CIP folks outside of their assigned mentor is encouraged.
  • Project fellows generally create something from scratch, while educator fellows use existing CIP resources as a jumping off point to create something for their community.

Can educators apply for the project track?

Educators can apply for either track. If you’re an educator who would like to make something from scratch under the guidance of a CIP mentor with specific expertise — such as in public libraries, data and misinformation, health misinformation, etc. — you should apply for the project track. If you are an educator who would like to use CIP resources and models as a starting point to create something tailored to your community under the guidance of the CIP Education & Engagement manager, you should choose the educator track.

CIP community fellow, Fulbright scholar team up to test educational card game at Seattle’s Ballard High School

Ballard High School teacher Shawn Lee, a member of the 2024-25 CIP Community Fellowship cohort, teamed up with visiting Fulbright Scholar Scott DeJong to co-develop The Feed: A Game of Social Media Mischief, a card game for high school students that explores the various motivations and incentives around sharing social media content. Their collaboration is a great example of how pairing insights from academic research and professional practice can lead to deeper community connection and novel approaches to media literacy education. Learn more in a Q&A interview.

High school students, local seniors in Sedro-Woolley demonstrate the power of intergenerational learning

The June 2024 “Leveling Up Seniors” event, co-organized by the Center for an Informed Public, Sedro-Woolley High School teachers and librarians (including 2023-24 CIP Community Fellowship cohort member Linsey Kitchens), Central Skagit Library System, and the Sedro-Woolley Senior Center, not only shared important lessons around technology, social media, and information literacy skills, but also fostered valuable community connections between older and younger generations.
