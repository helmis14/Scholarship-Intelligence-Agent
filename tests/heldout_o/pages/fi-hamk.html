Tuition Fees and Scholarships

Here you can find information concerning the tuition fees and scholarships of Häme University of Applied Sciences (HAMK).

Information in a nutshell:

Tuition fee for Bachelor’s Degree: 9 700€ / Academic Year

Tuition fee for Master’s Degree: 11 700€ / Academic Year

Scholarship based on Finnish Language Skills. No full-scholarships!

Early Bird Discount available, if first year payment is made in time.

Do I need to pay the Tuition fee?

EU / EEA Students

There are no tuition fee for citizens of Finland, the European Union (EU) and the European Economic Area (EEA) countries, or citizens of Switzerland.

Citizens of EU/ EEA: Please notice that you need to to submit a proof of your citizenship to Admissions services. Please submit a copy of your valid passport, valid national ID card, or valid identity card where your citizenship is indicated as an attachment to your application.

Citizens of Finland are not required to provide proof of their citizenship as it will be verified from the population register.

Students coming Outside of EU/EEA area

Yes, you need to pay the tuition fee to be able to study in HAMK and Finland. Please check the information from below.

Important information: After the legislative change 1 December 2024 those switching from a study-based residence permit to an employment-based residence permit are no longer exempt from paying tuition fee.

Holding a residence permit in another EU/EEA country does not exempt a student from the tuition fee.

There will be no tuition fee for non-EU/EEA students

  • who study in degree programmes taught in Finnish
  • who have an EU Blue Card granted in Finland
  • who have a continuous residence permit card in Finland, type A permit, and a person (or a family member) has not been granted a residence permit to Finland for the first time on the basis of studies
    • You are also exempted if you have a family-ties permit based on your spouse’s permanent residence or EU citizenship. In addition, a continuous residence permit card in Finland (type A), may exempt you from paying tuition fees if it is granted on grounds other than studying. However, you are required to pay tuition fees in the following cases:
      • If your first residence permit in Finland was granted for the purpose of studying, you are required to pay tuition fees, even if you later obtained a continuous residence permit (type A) on other grounds.
      • If you have a continuous residence permit (type A) based on family ties, but your first permit was granted for studies, and your family member currently also has a continuous resident permit (type A) and they originally came to Finland on a residence permit granted for studying, you are also required to pay tuition fees.
      • If your continuous residence permit (type A) is granted for studying, you are required to pay tuition fees.
    • Tuition fee liability is checked from all applicants, and permit history will be confirmed from Finnish Immigration Service (Migri).
    • NB: Type A permit granted for studying in Finland (from 15 April 2022 onwards) doesn’t exempt student from paying tuition fee.
  • who have a permanent residence permit card in Finland, type P permit
  • who have an EU Family Member’s Residence Card in Finland
  • who have an EU residence permit for third-country citizens with long-term residence permit card in Finland (Type P-EU)
  • who have a Brexit residence permit card, Type SEU = Right of residence under the withdrawal agreement
  • who have a residence permit card on the basis of temporary protection or asylum.
    • NOTE! Students with a residence permit based on asylum or international protection are not exempt from tuition fees if their first residence permit in Finland was issued for studies. Tuition fee exemption applies only if the student’s first residence permit in Finland was granted on the basis of asylum or international protection. 

If you are unsure about whether you need to pay tuition fees or not, check the ‘Am I required to pay tuition fees?‘ section at Studyinfo.fi. 

Providing the documents 

Students are required to provide one of the following documents to be exempted from paying tuition fees: 

  • EU Blue Card granted in Finland
  • Continuous residence permit card in Finland, Type A permit (if a person has not been granted a residence permit to Finland for the first time on the basis of studies)
  • NB: Type A permit granted for studying in Finland (from 15 April 2022 onwards) doesn’t exempt student from paying tuition fee.
  • EU Family Member’s Residence Card in Finland
  • Permanent residence permit card in Finland, Type P permit
  • EU residence permit for third-country citizens with long term residence permit card in Finland (Type P-EU)
  • Who have a Brexit residence permit card, Type SEU = Right of residence under the withdrawal agreement
  • Who have a residence permit card on the basis of temporary protection

Please notice: if you receive one of the documents mentioned above at any point of the admission process or while studying, please provide us the document immediately via this link:

Submit your documents

In order to be eligible for exemption from the tuition fee, new student must submit a new residence permit before the start of the academic year (no later than July 31) to be eligible for exemption from the tuition fee for the next academic year.

Continuing students must submit a new residence permit before the start of next semester, December 31 (for spring semester) or July 31 (for autumn semester)

Legislative Changes 1 December 2024: Changing Study-Based Residence Permit to an Employment-Based Residence Permit

The Finnish Government has made important changes to the Universities of Applied Sciences Act, which will affect international students in Finland who will apply for possible tuition fee exemptions starting from 1 December 2024.

  1. After 1 December 2024, students who receive a new continuous residence permit (type A) for an employed person will not be exempt from tuition fees if their first residence permit in Finland was based on studying.

2. A student who has been exempted from tuition fees before 1 December 2024 will remain exempted at least until the residence permit in which the exemption is based on expires. However, your tuition fee liability may be reassessed in certain circumstances, as described below.

Please note that:

  • If there are gaps between the residence permits, student’s obligation to pay the tuition fee will be reassessed
  • If the new residence permit is issued on different grounds than the original residence permit, the student’s obligation to pay the tuition fee will be reassessed
  • If a student has been previously exempted from paying tuition fee and they apply for re-granting of study right (Application based on an uncompleted degree in higher education) the payment obligation is re-evaluated. More information on Planning Your Studies – HAMK
  • If a student loses their right to study due to neglecting their academic year enrolment (or failing to pay the tuition fee), and later applies for reinstatement of their study right, their tuition fee liability will be reassessed under the new legislation. (In such cases, the student may become liable to pay tuition fees even if they were previously exempt from them).
  • If changes occur during the studies at the student’s own initiative, e.g. a change of degree title or a transfer between universities, the student’s tuition fee liability will be reassessed under the new legislation
  • Pending residence permit applications do not qualify for exemption.

Living Costs and Financing Your Studies

All students at HAMK must ensure they have sufficient funds to cover tuition fees (if applicable) and living expenses for the entire duration of their studies. Before arriving in Finland, make sure you have a realistic financial plan. 

Note: International students who come to Finland solely for their studies are generally not eligible for government financial aid for students. Read more from Kela’s pages. 

The cost of living in Finland varies based on individual lifestyle, location, and housing choices. As a student, we recommend budgeting approximately 950–1,200 EUR per month to cover essential expenses. Below is an estimated breakdown of typical student costs: 

  • Rent: 400–650 EUR 
  • Food: 400–500 EUR 
  • Other expenses (leisure, internet, phone, etc.): 100 EUR 

Tip: Students in Finland can benefit from a variety of student discounts on transport, dining, and leisure activities. For example, train tickets can be 30–50% cheaper for students. To access these discounts, you need a valid student card. Learn more about student union membership and student card benefits here. 

International exchange and degree students are allowed to work in Finland with certain restrictions. Students from outside the EU/EEA can work up to 30 hours per week on average during the academic year. However, finding a part-time job can be challenging, especially in the first two years, due to language barriers and competition. Even in roles requiring minimal Finnish skills, job opportunities are limited. 

While internships at HAMK are mandatory and work experience is valuable, we encourage students to prioritize their studies, make meaningful connections, and fully embrace student life. It’s also important to note that job availability varies significantly between campuses. Do not rely on finding a part-time job immediately upon arrival—students should have sufficient financial resources to support their studies without one. 

Additionally, work should not interfere with your studies. Balancing coursework with part-time jobs can be demanding, and wages often do not fully cover living expenses, especially for students paying tuition fees. Learning Finnish can improve job prospects, but it’s crucial to set realistic expectations regarding work opportunities. 

Finally, if you work in Finland, you must pay taxes. Before starting employment, you need to apply for a tax card and/or tax number from the Finnish Tax Office (Vero). 

By planning ahead and understanding these challenges, you can better navigate student life in Finland. 

Scholarships

At HAMK we don’t offer any fully funded scholarship. HAMK’s scholarship programme is available for the nationals of non-EU and non-EEA countries who are studying for a degree in HAMK’s English-taught degree programmes. The nationals of non-EU and non-EEA countries are obliged to pay the annual tuition fee.

How the programme works?

HAMK grants scholarships based on Finnish language skills and study progress.

The scholarship programme awards students who gain at least the basic skills of Finnish language during their degree studies and simultaneously advance in their studies at HAMK. 

The aim of HAMK’s scholarship programme is to support the future employment of international degree students in Finland by encouraging students to gain good level of Finnish language during their studies.

Students can be awarded with a scholarships two times during their studies, based on their level of Finnish language  (A2 and B1) and provided that their studies progress with 60 ECTS credits per every academic year enrolled as present at HAMK. The Finnish language skills are evaluated by language test results.

  1. Scholarship: Basic User of Finnish language (CEFR level-A2): €3 200
    • Paid once, the earliest after the first academic year .
    • Bachelor´s degree students have the opportunity to gain level A2 by taking part in Finnish language studies offered by HAMK and studying independently.
  2. Scholarship: Independent User of Finnish language – (CEFR level B1): €6 500
    • Paid once, the earliest after the second academic year.
    • If the student is awarded with Scholarship 1, they can be awarded with Scholarship 2 after the second academic year, at the earliest.
    • HAMK encourages students to aim to level B1 and will support and offer guidance on how to achieve the level needed. B1 is an advanced level requiring long-term commitment to studying Finnish.

Language level assessment is based on the Common European Framework of Reference for Languages: Learning, teaching, assessment (CEFR).

You can check the language levels’ requirements from here.

Student can be awarded with a one scholarship at the end of their studies, based on their level of Finnish language and provided that their studies progress with 45 ECTS credits per academic year at HAMK. The Finnish language skills are evaluated by language test results.

Scholarship: Basic User of Finnish language (CEFR level-A2): €2 500

  • Paid once, the earliest after the first academic year
  • There are no Finnish language studies included in HAMK’s Master’s degree programmes. Students have an opportunity to take part in Finnish language studies offered for Bachelor students (10 credits) to gain the level A2. These studies are voluntary and free of charge. To receive the scholarship, the language skills need to be proven by submitting one of the approved test results.

Language level assessment is based on the Common European Framework of Reference for Languages: Learning, teaching, assessment (CEFR).

You can check the requirements from here.

Register for the test in accordance with its enrollment timeline. You register for the test in Pakki. HAMK manages the registration process, but the test itself is organized by Vocational College Tavastia.

  • Please note that your registration is binding. The last day to register for the test is also the last day to cancel your registration. If you do not cancel your registration before the registration closes, HAMK will count it as if you took part in the test.
  • Only tuition fee-paying students, enrolled as present, who have completed at least 60 credits during their studies at HAMK can register for the A2 level test. If you are a first year student or registered as absent, you cannot attend. Please note that you are allowed to participate in the language test even if you do not fulfill all scholarship requirements, yet.
  • If you failed your first test, you cannot participate without paying for all future retakes. Only the first test on A2 level is free of charge. Payment information: see below.

Payment

The first test is free of charge, but if you need to retake the test, you will have to pay for the retake. The price is 100 euros for students who need to retake HAMK’s own A2 level test. Retaking the test means you need to do the whole test. If you need to pay for the test, note that it needs to be paid through HAMK Shop before the registration ends.

How to prepare for the test?

What to bring with you to the language test, and how to prepare for it:

  • Arrive well before the test is scheduled to start. Arriving late means you will fail the test.
  • Computers and headphones will be provided for you – you do not need to bring your own.
  • Do not use your mobile phone, smartwatch, AI glasses, personal headphones, or other electronic devices during the test. Put these items in your bag or in your coat pocket, away from you. Tavastia’s staff members are allowed to remove prohibited items during the test.
  • All questions about the test are to be sent to tavastiakoulutus@kktavastia.fi. The test is organized by Vocational College Tavastia, and HAMK only manages the registration in Pakki. 

Note that HAMK offers an online course for those of you preparing for the A2 level language test: KM00FC00-3003 Suomi kuntoon! Prep your Finnish for the A2 level test.

The main parts of the test’s structure:

  • The test will include the following parts. See the requirements in level A2 and B1 on the assessment grid of The Common European Framework of Reference for Languages and CEFR Language Levels: A1, A2, B1, B2, C1, C2 Explained
  1. Speaking
  2. Listening comprehension
  3. Reading comprehension
  4. Writing

Demanded result to pass the A2 level language test

To pass the language test, you must score at least 30 points out of a possible 40 and obtain a minimum of six points in each individual section. In addition, you must earn points from all the individual test sections so that your total score is at least 30 points. Cheating, or the use of prohibited items during the test, will lead to a failed test result. Note that this means that you will have to pay for all future A2 level language tests yourself. 

You will receive your result directly in your Pakki once Vocational College Tavastia has sent the results to HAMK.

Assessment criteria

Speaking Assessment (A2 Level)

  • Pronounces words and speaks clearly enough for the listener to understand the main content of the speech.
  • Uses everyday vocabulary and some idiomatic expressions.
  • Uses several simple idiomatic expressions and occasional more advanced idioms.
  • Answers questions comprehensibly and responds to what is actually being asked.
  • Provides an appropriate amount of speech. The expectation is that the student answers questions with a level of detail that is appropriate to the question asked.

Listening Comprehension Assessment (A2 Level)

  • Can follow the main points of clearly articulated factual speech at a general level.
  • Can identify the topic of a conversation taking place around them.
  • Understands common vocabulary used in speech about familiar topics and general knowledge.
  • Can respond appropriately in everyday situations or customer service interactions.

Reading Comprehension Assessment Criteria (A2 Level)

  • Understands the main points and some details of messages consisting of a few paragraphs in moderately demanding everyday contexts.
  • Can acquire new information on familiar topics.
  • Can infer the meanings of unfamiliar words from context.

Writing Assessment Criteria (A2 Level)

  • Can cope with routine everyday situations through writing.
  • Can write very short, simple descriptions of events, past activities, personal experiences, or everyday aspects of their living environment (e.g., short letters, notes, applications, and telephone messages).
  • Has command of basic everyday vocabulary, grammatical structures, and the most common linking devices.
  • Writes simple words and structures correctly, but makes errors in less common structures and forms and may produce awkward expressions.
  • Writes as extensively and relevantly as possible according to the task instructions.

YKI tests are arranged by the Finnish National Agency for Education (EDUFI): Information on YKI tests

If you as a tuition fee-paying student wish to prove your language skills in Finnish to receive a scholarship, you can complete the National Certificates of Language Proficiency (YKI) test. By passing the intermediate level YKI test you are eligible for the scholarship based on B1 level language skills.

You register for the intermediate/B1 level YKI test yourself. When you register for the YKI intermediate level test for the first time and you take the test, HAMK will reimburse the test fee to you. You must provide the receipt and the test result. You can apply for reimbursement after you have participated in the test, it does not matter if you pass or fail the test – the first test’s fee will be reimbursed.
If you do not pass the YKI intermediate level test on your first attempt, you will need to pay for all later tests yourself.

No other test results than the intermediate level YKI test’s results can be used as grounds for receiving the B1 level scholarship.

Please note that HAMK cannot contact the YKI test organization in any matter, as the organization administering the YKI tests is operating on a national level.

Process for reimbursement of your first YKI intermediate level test

Here is the process for how to be reimbursed for the YKI test fee:

  1.  You register to a YKI intermediate test, in a city and on a date that works for you.
  2. You pay the YKI test fee 190 euros yourself.
  3. Save the receipt for the payment! You will need it later.
  4. You participate in the YKI intermediate level test.
  5. You wait for the official YKI test result.
  6. When you have received the official test result (your result can be either pass or fail, the reimbursement of your first YKI B1 test is not affected by your result) – note that you need to save that test result!
  7. You send both the receipt and the test result to Study Services via an e-form (if you only send the receipt then no reimbursement can be done)
  8. Study Services registers your first participation in the YKI intermediate level test and reimburses you the 190 euros. HAMK will need your bank details, preferably a Finnish bank account.
  9. Any language test on B1 level taken after this: you will have to pay for it yourself. Only the first test on B1 level is free of charge.

YKI test result requirements

You need to complete one of the subtest combinations below at the required level of proficiency:

Level B1 (intermediate level)

To receive the B1 level scholarship, you need to complete YKI’s intermediate level test with a level 3 proficiency.

  • speaking and writing: level of proficiency 3 OR
  • listening comprehension and writing: level of proficiency 3 OR
  • reading comprehension and speaking: level of proficiency 3

Submit your scholarship application and/or test reimbursement application through our e-form.

As of August 2026 HAMK does not reimburse A2 level YKI tests, but they are accepted as proof of Finnish language skills.
