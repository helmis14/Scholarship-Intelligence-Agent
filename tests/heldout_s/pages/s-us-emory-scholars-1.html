Making College Affordable

Emory offers one of the nation’s most generous financial aid programs to help students attend college. For first-degree domestic undergraduates, need-based loans have been replaced with institutional grants. And students from families earning up to $200,000, with typical assets, qualify for free tuition.

Emory Advantage Plus

To qualify, students must be domestic, first-degree undergraduates eligible for federal financial aid (i.e., US citizens) enrolled at least half time during the fall and spring in one of Emory’s four undergraduate schools: Emory College, Oxford College, Goizueta Business School, or the School of Nursing.

Families must have total earnings of up to $200,000, based on FAFSA and CSS Profile, with typical assets. Students must qualify for need-based aid, which is reviewed annually. The program covers up to eight semesters of tuition (fall and spring only).

No additional application is required for the Emory Advantage Plus program. Undergraduate students and their families should follow Emory’s standard financial aid application process by submitting the FAFSA and CSS Profile. 

Learn more about Emory Advantage Plus

Cost of Attendance

The term “cost of attendance” refers to an estimate of the total amount it will cost to attend college for one academic period. This includes tuition and fees, on-campus room and board (or housing and food costs for students living off-campus), and costs for books, supplies, and transportation. Learn more about the cost of attendance for your undergraduate school or program at Emory.

Types of Financial Aid

From federal, state, and private aid to institutional grants and scholarships, learn more about financial aid options.

Explore Undergraduate Financial Aid

  • Grants and Scholarships

    These funding sources do not need to be repaid. Amounts can be determined based on demonstrated need or academic merit.

  • Educational Loans

    Can provide additional funding support after scholarships and grants are applied. These loans require repayment once enrollment drops below half time.

  • Scholar Programs

    Scholarships, dedicated programming, and distinct opportunities for exceptional incoming first-year students chosen as Woodruff and Goizueta Scholars.

  • Work Study

    Need-based awards that provide paid on-campus employment crucial for gaining work experience and assisting with educational expenses.

  • International Students

    Each year we offer need-based financial aid packages to a select group of international students.

  • Veterans

    We offer college funding sources and options for students and families receiving veterans’ education benefits.

  • Questbridge

    A national program that identifies high-achieving, low-income students and connects them with the nation’s best colleges and universities.
