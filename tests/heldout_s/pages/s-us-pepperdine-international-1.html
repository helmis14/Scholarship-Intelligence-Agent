Scholarships

Scholarships are available for new and continuing students.  New students may receive up to $15,000 in new student scholarships (Education for Life, Merit, Pathway to Purpose) and additional awards through other funding sources (Give a Gift, GSEP Urban Initiative Scholarship, E.V. Pulias Endowed Scholarship, Olaf H. Tegner Endowed Scholarship).

A scholarship is financial aid that does not need to be repaid. Total aid awarded in loans, grants, and scholarships may not exceed a student's total Cost of Attendance. Additional details and a link to the scholarship applications can be found below.

 

Types of Scholarships

 Pepperdine Scholarships for Continuing Students

For GSEP Pepperdine Scholarships, continuing students are defined as students presently enrolled at Pepperdine University's Graduate School of Education and Psychology in their second term or more in the same academic program. Please note, that transfers from a GSEP Doctor of Education program to the GSEP Doctor of Philosophy in Global Leadership and Change program are considered continuing students for Pepperdine scholarship purposes.

Awards are made based on a variety of criteria, including academic merit, financial need, specific academic programs, and the promotion of cultural diversity at the school.

Please note: Students in the 100% online (Psychology @ Pepperdine) programs are only eligible for the Curtice & Evelyn Booth Veterans Endowed Scholarship and the Waves Got Your Six Scholarship. These scholarships can be combined with other scholarships except Federal and Institutional Military Scholarships/Benefits, Athletic Scholarships, and the GSEP Diversity Scholarship. Students receiving these scholarships/benefits, or other forms of third-party scholarship(s) will require further approvals. Scholarships are generally awarded at the start of the academic year and students must be enrolled full-time (6 units each term) to remain eligible.

View GSEP Pepperdine Scholarship Policies

GSEP Urban Initiative Scholarship

Eligibility Criteria

The GSEP Urban Initiative Scholarship applies to both currently enrolled and new students and is designed to increase graduate-level higher education access and to promote academic excellence among students engaging with and using their education to support urban communities. This fund seeks to reduce financial barriers such students and increase Pepperdine's involvement in urban community activities.

Application Process

The applicant must submit an essay and letter of recommendation from a community leader, demonstrate a strong commitment to Christian values, and be actively engaged in both the church and the broader community. The essay should be approximately 300 words and address one of the following two points:
(1)  Please describe how a particular activity or program you are actively engaged in within urban communities, represents the Pepperdine Mission. 
(2)  How does your academic work/dissertation topic or research interest seek to promote academic excellence among students engaging with urban communities?
As an alternative to the essay, applicants may submit a personal video message expounding on their involvement with an urban community, and how the university's mission is supported in these efforts. 

Applications must be submitted electronically to the following link: Urban Initiative Scholarship Application

Governing Policies

  • This $2,500 - $5,000 scholarship is intended to support one academic year of study and will be allocated evenly across all terms.
  • The awarded scholarship will be forfeited at the point when the awardee advances through the academic program and completes the coursework.
  • If there is a leave of absence, the awardee will no longer be eligible for funding and must reapply for consideration.
  • Recipients of this scholarship are not restricted from receiving other scholarships for which they qualify.

 

GSEP Endowed Board of Visitors Scholarship

Eligibility Criteria:

The GSEP Endowed Board of Visitors Scholarship applies to both currently enrolled and new students and is established to provide lived leadership lessons for a doctoral student matriculating in one of the GSEP doctoral programs. The recipient must be in good academic standing and is required to serve as a student representative on the Board and to assist the Secretary of the Board in preparing minutes and other communications for its members. Meetings are held at various locations. The scholarship is for one academic year.

Application Process:

The applicant must submit an essay (approximately 300 words) and three letters of recommendation from church and community leaders. The essay should address the applicant's eligibility with particular emphasis on their ongoing involvement in and contributions to the church and the broader community. It should address their experience with technology and explain their planned technology/leadership project. Scholarships can be renewed annually with proof of good academic standing and an approved technology/leadership project plan. Applications must be submitted electronically to the following link: GSEP Endowed Board of Visitors Scholarship

Administration Policies:

  1. The application deadline is June 15th. A deadline extension can only be approved by the GSEP Dean.
  2. This scholarship is optimally designed to support one (1) year of coursework: Three (3) terms totaling $3,000.00.
  3. This scholarship is designed to cover the coursework and comprehensive exam phase of the academic program.
  4. The dissertation phase of the academic program is not covered by the scholarship.
  5. The awarded scholarship will be forfeited at the point when the awardee advances through the academic program, completes the coursework, and begins the dissertation process.
  6. Undisbursed funds will be added to the interest accrued by the endowment. They will be distributed during subsequent awarding periods in late July or early August.
  7. The scholarship will not be held for awardees who take a leave of absence.
  8. Recipients of this scholarship are not restricted from receiving other scholarships for which they qualify.

 

Curtice & Evelyn Booth Veterans Endowed Scholarship

Eligibility Criteria:

Awarded to both currently enrolled and new students who are veterans, active-duty reservists, or dependents (children or spouses) of a veteran or active-duty reservist. Recipients must demonstrate excellent academic performance (cumulative GPA of 3.5 or above) and must be pursuing a master's or doctoral degree in any format (traditional, hybrid, and online) at the Graduate School of Education and Psychology. This fund seeks to reduce financial barriers for this targeted population, either towards tuition or academic balances, or other ancillary costs. This is awarded for one term only.

If you qualify based on the criteria above, please complete the following steps:

  • Once the General Application is completed, you will find the scholarship application under "Other Recommended Opportunities" or "Opportunities."

Students are notified via email with instructions to apply when the application is available.

 

David Levy Endowed Scholarship for Excellence in Critical Thinking

Eligibility Criteria:

The Dr. David A. Levy Scholarship for Excellence in Critical Thinking applies to both currently enrolled and new students and is intended for graduate students enrolled in the psychology program at the Graduate School of Education and Psychology with a minimum grade point average of 3.5. The recipient must demonstrate excellence in critical thinking, defined as a set of systematic cognitive strategies to examine, evaluate, and understand events, solve problems, and make decisions on the basis of logical reasoning and valid evidence. Components of critical thinking include: maintaining an attitude that is both open-minded and skeptical; minimizing personal biases; recognizing the distinction between facts and theories; differentiating dichotomous variables from continuous variables; understanding causal relationships; identifying similarities and differences between phenomena; objectively gathering, and weighing, and synthesizing information; forming reasonable inferences; striving for factual accuracy and logical consistency; questioning underlying assumptions and beliefs; discerning hidden or implicit values; exploring alternative perspectives and explanations; searching for creative solutions; and developing a tolerance for uncertainty and ambiguity. 

Application Process:

If you qualify based on the criteria above, please complete the following steps:

  • Please visit https://pepperdine.academicworks.com/users/sign_in. Once on the page, kindly click "Sign In With Your Institution." You must use your Pepperdine Network ID and password. Note: Do not click the "Opportunities" tab on the upper left-hand side.
  • You must complete the General Application if you have not done so already.
  • Once the General Application is completed, you will find the scholarship application under "Other Recommended Opportunities" or "Opportunities.

Within the application, students are asked to submit a brief essay of two to three pages, double-spaced with 12-point font in Times New Roman. Applicants will be required describe how they’ve come to value and apply their critical thinking skills during their academic and personal development. They are encouraged to provide specific examples from their own life. This is explicitly a merit scholarship, rather than predicated on financial need or applicants’ demographic characteristics.

The Director of the Pat Lucas Center-in coordination with the MA in Teaching leadership will identify & review qualified candidates.

 

Marco Garcia Memorial Fellowship

Qualified applicants are invited to apply for a scholarship in the amount of $1,500. Several scholarships are available. In addition, the possibility of continued funding for subsequent years is available for a limited number of recipients.

The Enrique and Lucy Garcia Family Foundation established the scholarship in memory of their beloved son, Marco Garcia (1973-1995), who noticed a substantial need for more Mexican-American mental health professionals. The scholarship was instituted to celebrate and remember Marco’s life, spirit, and vision.

Their oldest son was a very talented and gifted artist and musician devoted to social activism. He was a much-loved son, brother, grandson, nephew, cousin, uncle, and friend who touched many people differently. Marco was a free-spirited, caring, and loving person who enjoyed skateboarding and snowboarding. He was the type of person who would have given a stranger the “shirt off his back” or his “last dime” because “they needed it more than me,” as Marco would say.

Marco made quite an impact and touched many people during his short life, and he continues to do so in spirit. It is hoped that the Marco Garcia Memorial Fellowship will help him continue to “touch” and impact the lives of others while helping to fill a need in the mental health field.

Eligibility Criteria:

This scholarship is available to students enrolled full-time in either the PsyD in Clinical Psychology or MA in Clinical Psychology (Daytime Format, Evening Format, or Aliento) programs and is intended for those who aspire to use their education in psychology to support the mental health of the Latino/a/x community. Mexican or Mexican-American applicants are encouraged to apply. Students anticipated to graduate this term or in the upcoming term are ineligible to apply for this scholarship. Recipients must also demonstrate excellent academic performance (cumulative GPA of 3.5 or above). 

Application Process:

Submit a three-page (single-spaced) essay describing:

  • Personal background
  • Professional and volunteer experience
  • Issues facing the Latino/a community
  • How your work as a mental health professional will address the issues facing the Latino/a community
  • Include one letter of recommendation from a Pepperdine University faculty member or clinical supervisor

Application submission through Blackbaud Awards Management

 Pepperdine Scholarships for New Students

A new student for GSEP Pepperdine Scholarships is defined as a student in his/her first term at Pepperdine University's Graduate School of Education and Psychology (GSEP). Students who are making a program plan change, modality change, or have completed a GSEP Doctor of Education program and are now applying to the GSEP Doctor of Philosophy in Global Leadership and Change program are not considered new students for scholarship purposes.

Awards are made based on a variety of criteria, including academic merit, financial need, specific academic programs, and the promotion of cultural competency at the school. Note: Scholarship funds are limited and are awarded based on this criteria and on a first-come, first-served basis or until all funds have been distributed.

Please Note: Students in the online format programs are only eligible for the GSEP Urban Initiative Scholarship, Curtice & Evelyn Booth Veterans Endowed Scholarship, and the Waves Got Your Six Scholarship. Students in the online EDD program are also eligible for the GSEP Greater Purpose Scholarship.  Scholarships are generally awarded at the start of the academic year and students must be enrolled full-time (6 units each term) to remain eligible. These scholarships can be combined with other scholarships except for the Colleagues Grant, Federal and Institutional Military Scholarships/Benefits, Athletic Scholarships, and GSEP Greater Purpose Scholarship. Students receiving these scholarships/benefits, or other forms of third-party scholarship(s) will require further approvals.

Pathway to Purpose Scholarships: $1,000-$7,000 (one-time award)

Eligibility Criteria:

This Pathway to Purpose scholarship is designed to support new, first-time GSEP students on their pathway to a life of purpose, service, and leadership through earning a graduate degree at Pepperdine University Graduate School of Education and Psychology.

GSEP is dedicated to building scholar-practitioners through hands-on learning.  We recognize experience and contributions within your community are as important as your academic background when preparing for a graduate program.  We also recognize the financial impact of pursuing a graduate degree and advancing your pathway to purpose. The Pathway to Purpose scholarship was created to provide financial support to first-time students who are actively engaged in community or volunteer work, service to diverse and underserved populations, or face a significant financial challenge.

The Pathway to Purpose scholarship is awarded over three academic terms and a 3.0 GPA or higher must be maintained during that time. Scholarship amounts range from $1,000 to $7,000. Once accepted to a qualifying GSEP degree program, candidates may be eligible for the Pathway to Purpose scholarship.  

Application Process:

Applicants are asked to write a 250-300 word essay on one of the three following prompts:  1) Contribution to Diversity or Service of Underrepresented Groups, 2) Community Service or Volunteer Work, or 3) Financial Need or Burden. Follow this link to apply.

Note: Pathway to Purpose Scholarships do not apply to applicants who are applying for the following programs:

  • MA in Clinical Psychology with an emphasis in Marriage and Family Therapy (Daytime Format)

GSEP Urban Initiative Scholarship

Eligibility Criteria

The GSEP Urban Initiative Scholarship applies to both currently enrolled and new students and is designed to increase graduate-level higher education access and to promote academic excellence among students engaging with and using their education to support urban communities. This fund seeks to reduce financial barriers such students and increase Pepperdine's involvement in urban community activities. 

Application Process

The applicant must submit an essay and letter of recommendation from a community leader, demonstrate a strong commitment to Christian values, and be actively engaged in both the church and the broader community. The essay should be approximately 300 words and address one of the following two points:
(1)  Please describe how a particular activity or program you are actively engaged in within urban communities, represents the Pepperdine Mission. 
(2)  How does your academic work/dissertation topic or research interest seek to promote academic excellence among students engaging with urban communities?

As an alternative to the essay, applicants may submit a personal video message expounding on their involvement with an urban community, and how the university's mission is supported in these efforts. 

Applications must be submitted electronically to the following link: Urban Initiative Scholarship Application

Governing Policies

  • This $2,500 - $5,000 scholarship is intended to support one academic year of study and will be allocated evenly across all terms.
  • The awarded scholarship will be forfeited at the point when the awardee advances through the academic program and completes the coursework.
  • If there is a leave of absence, the awardee will no longer be eligible for funding and must reapply for consideration.
  • Recipients of this scholarship are not restricted from receiving other scholarships for which they qualify.

 

GSEP Endowed Board of Visitors Scholarship

Eligibility Criteria:

The GSEP Endowed Board of Visitors Scholarship applies to both currently enrolled and new students and is established to provide lived leadership lessons for a doctoral student matriculating in one of the GSEP doctoral programs. The recipient must be in good academic standing and is required to serve as a student representative on the Board and to assist the Secretary of the Board in preparing minutes and other communications for its members. Meetings are held at various locations. The scholarship is for one academic year.

Application Process:

The applicant must submit an essay (approximately 300 words) and three letters of recommendation from church and community leaders. The essay should address the applicant's eligibility with particular emphasis on their ongoing involvement in and contributions to the church and the broader community. It should address their experience with technology and explain their planned technology/leadership project. Scholarships can be renewed annually with proof of good academic standing and an approved technology/leadership project plan. Applications must be submitted electronically to the following link: The GSEP Endowed Board of Visitors Scholarship.

Administration Policies:

  1. The application deadline is June 15th. A deadline extension can only be approved by the GSEP Dean.
  2. This scholarship is optimally designed to support one (1) year of coursework: Three (3) terms totaling $3,000.00.
  3. This scholarship is designed to cover the coursework and comprehensive exam phase of the academic program.
  4. The dissertation phase of the academic program is not covered by the scholarship.
  5. The awarded scholarship will be forfeited at the point when the awardee advances through the academic program, completes the coursework, and begins the dissertation process.
  6. Undisbursed funds will be added to the interest accrued by the endowment. They will be distributed during subsequent awarding periods in late July or early August.
  7. The scholarship will not be held for awardees who take a leave of absence.
  8. Recipients of this scholarship are not restricted from receiving other scholarships for which they qualify.

 

David Levy Scholarship for Excellence in Critical Thinking

Eligibility Criteria

The Dr. David A. Levy Scholarship for Excellence in Critical Thinking applies to both currently enrolled and new students and is intended for graduate students enrolled in the psychology program at the Graduate School of Education and Psychology with a minimum grade point average of 3.5. The recipient must demonstrate excellence in critical thinking, defined as a set of systematic cognitive strategies to examine, evaluate, and understand events, solve problems, and make decisions on the basis of logical reasoning and valid evidence. Components of critical thinking include: maintaining an attitude that is both open-minded and skeptical; minimizing personal biases; recognizing the distinction between facts and theories; differentiating dichotomous variables from continuous variables; understanding causal relationships; identifying similarities and differences between phenomena; objectively gathering, and weighing, and synthesizing information; forming reasonable inferences; striving for factual accuracy and logical consistency; questioning underlying assumptions and beliefs; discerning hidden or implicit values; exploring alternative perspectives and explanations; searching for creative solutions; and developing a tolerance for uncertainty and ambiguity. 

Application Process:

If you qualify based on the criteria above, please complete the following steps:

  • Please visit https://pepperdine.academicworks.com/users/sign_in. Once on the page, kindly click "Sign In With Your Institution." You must use your Pepperdine Network ID and password. Note: Do not click the "Opportunities" tab on the upper left-hand side.
  • You must complete the General Application if you have not done so already.
  • Once the General Application is completed, you will find the scholarship application under "Other Recommended Opportunities" or "Opportunities."

Within the application, students are asked to submit a brief essay of two to three pages, double-spaced with 12-point font in Times New Roman. Applicants will be required describe how they’ve come to value and apply their critical thinking skills during their academic and personal development. They are encouraged to provide specific examples from their own life. This is explicitly a merit scholarship, rather than predicated on financial need or applicants’ demographic characteristics.
